About Black In Education

Our Mission

Black In Education is an innovative AI-powered platform designed to address the unique challenges faced by Black students and professionals in the education sector. We're committed to closing the achievement gap, increasing representation, and fostering a supportive community.

Our AI technology provides personalized learning paths, career guidance, and networking opportunities tailored to the needs of Black individuals in education.

Current Trial

We're excited to announce our ongoing trial in partnership with:

  • Unison Union
  • University of Bedminster

This trial allows us to refine our AI algorithms, gather valuable feedback, and demonstrate the real-world impact of our platform.

Our Programs

Self Program

Tailored recommendations for books, courses, and articles based on your interests and career goals. Track progress and adjust dynamically.

Our AI technology helps address the achievement gap, where only 17.3% of Black students received a first-class degree, compared to 36.1% of White students in the UK.

Explore Self Program

Growth Program

Tailored recommendations for courses and certifications to develop new skills. Guidance on obtaining industry-recognized certifications.

We're addressing the underrepresentation in senior positions, where only 0.6% of Black academics are professors, compared to 11.9% of White academics in the UK.

Join Growth Program

Innovation Program

Access to AI-powered labs where students and staff can experiment with emerging technologies. Opportunities to work on real-world projects and solve practical problems.

We're addressing the underrepresentation in STEM fields, where only 6.2% of Black students are enrolled in STEM courses compared to 14.4% of White students in the UK.

Join Innovation Program

Community Program

AI-powered networking events to connect with peers and industry leaders. Social gatherings and activities to build a sense of community.

We're working to improve representation, as only 2.1% of academic staff in UK higher education institutions are Black, compared to 84.5% who are White.
